# Advanced Computer Networks: Overview of Wireless Technologies

## Introduction to Wireless Technologies
Wireless communication has revolutionized connectivity by eliminating the need for physical connections. Various wireless technologies serve different purposes and range from personal to global communication. This document provides an overview of WPAN, Bluetooth, VLC, ZigBee, WLAN, and GSM, along with their advantages and disadvantages.

---

## Wireless Personal Area Network (WPAN)
WPAN enables communication between devices in close proximity, typically within a range of 10 meters.

### Advantages
- Low power consumption.
- Cost-effective for short-range communication.
- Simplified device connectivity (e.g., wireless peripherals).

### Disadvantages
- Limited range and bandwidth.
- Susceptible to interference in crowded environments.

---

## Bluetooth
Bluetooth is a short-range wireless communication technology commonly used in WPANs to exchange data and connect devices.

### Advantages
- Widely supported and cost-effective.
- Robust against interference due to frequency hopping.
- Enables the creation of piconets and scatternets for device connectivity.

### Disadvantages
- Limited range (typically up to 10 meters, extendable in some cases).
- Moderate data rates compared to modern standards.
- Vulnerable to security threats such as unauthorized access.

---

## Visible Light Communication (VLC)
VLC uses visible light as a medium for communication, defined by IEEE 802.15.7 standards.

### Advantages
- High data rates in short-range communication.
- Immune to electromagnetic interference.
- Dual-use as lighting and communication.

### Disadvantages
- Requires line-of-sight for effective communication.
- Susceptible to obstructions and ambient light interference.
- Limited to indoor or controlled environments.

---

## ZigBee
ZigBee is a low-power, low-data-rate wireless communication technology, primarily used in embedded applications like home automation and IoT devices.

### Advantages
- Extremely low power consumption, suitable for battery-powered devices.
- Supports mesh networking, enhancing reliability and coverage.
- Cost-effective and easy to implement.

### Disadvantages
- Limited data rate (up to 250 kbps).
- Relatively short range, typically up to 100 meters.
- Not ideal for high-bandwidth applications.

---

## Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN)
WLAN enables wireless connectivity within a local area, commonly using Wi-Fi technology based on IEEE 802.11 standards.

### Advantages
- Flexibility in device placement and movement.
- Supports high data rates with modern standards (e.g., 802.11ac, 802.11ax).
- Cost-effective for local area coverage.

### Disadvantages
- Susceptible to interference from other wireless devices.
- Security concerns, requiring strong encryption protocols.
- Limited coverage area compared to wired networks.

---

## Global System for Mobile Communications (GSM)
GSM is a standard for cellular networks, offering voice, SMS, and data services.

### Advantages
- Wide coverage globally with extensive network infrastructure.
- Reliable for voice communication and low-data-rate applications.
- Supports roaming across regions and networks.

### Disadvantages
- Limited data rates compared to newer cellular technologies (e.g., LTE, 5G).
- High power consumption for mobile devices.
- Vulnerable to eavesdropping if encryption is weak or absent.
